Same underlying, same strike, same days to expiry, and the option costs twice as much. Why?

Implied volatility (IV): the size of move the market is pricing in.

In the Black-Scholes example (price 100, strike 100, 30 days), an at-the-money call costs about 1.7 at 15% IV and about 3.4 at 30% IV. Double the IV, roughly double the premium.

IV tends to rise before big events like results or elections, and drop sharply after. That drop can shrink an option's price even when you got the direction right.

Always check IV before you trade an option.
