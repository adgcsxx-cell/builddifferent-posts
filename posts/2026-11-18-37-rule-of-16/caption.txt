Implied volatility is quoted as a yearly number. That makes it hard to feel. The rule of 16 fixes that.

Divide IV by 16 to get the typical daily move the market is pricing in. Why 16? There are about 252 trading days in a year, and the square root of 252 is about 15.9.

→ IV 16%: about 1% a day
→ IV 32%: about 2% a day
→ One week at IV 16%: about 2.3%
→ One month at IV 16%: about 4.6%

Example: an index at 20,000 with IV of 16% is pricing a typical day of about 200 points.

Two warnings: it's one standard deviation, so bigger days still happen (markets have fat tails), and it says nothing about direction.

(Illustrative math, not a forecast.)
