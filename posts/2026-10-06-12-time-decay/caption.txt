Every option has an expiry date, and the clock never stops.

The part of an option's price that comes from time (its time value) shrinks every day, and not evenly: it speeds up as expiry gets closer.

In the simplified model shown, an at-the-money option still has about 48% of its time value with 7 days left, then loses all of it in the final week.

That's why buyers need the move to happen fast, and why sellers are paid to wait (while carrying the risk of a sharp move).

Next time you buy an option, check how many days you're paying for.
