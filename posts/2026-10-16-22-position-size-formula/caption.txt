Most traders pick a position size by feel: "I'll buy 500 shares." Professionals work it out backwards from the stop-loss.

Position size = (capital × risk %) ÷ (entry − stop)

Example: ₹10,00,000 capital, 1% risk = ₹10,000. Entry ₹500, stop ₹480, so ₹20 risk per share. Position: 10,000 ÷ 20 = 500 shares.

If the stop is hit, you lose about ₹10,000, exactly what you planned (plus costs).

A wider stop means fewer shares; a tighter stop means more. Your risk stays the same.
